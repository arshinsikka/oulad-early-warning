2. Specification
================

  Running variable      first-TMA score, centred at 40
  Outcome               not_completed (Section 3: Fail or Withdrawn)
  Primary bandwidth     +/- 10 marks
  Robustness            5, 8, 15, 20
  Estimator             local linear, triangular kernel, separate slopes each side
  Inference             robust bias-corrected (Calonico, Cattaneo, Titiunik), via rdrobust

The estimate is the limit from the right minus the limit from the left, so it is the effect of landing just ABOVE the pass mark. A negative coefficient means narrowly passing is associated with less non-completion.
rdrobust reports mass points in the running variable: scores are integers, so every attainable value is a mass point. That is a property of the data, not a defect of the fit, and it is handled by the estimator's mass-point adjustment. It matters for V1 below.

Conventional inference uses the point estimate and its own standard error. Bias-corrected recentres the estimate for the bias local linear fitting leaves behind; robust uses that recentred estimate with a variance that accounts for the correction, and it is the inference Section 12 pre-committed to. Where the three disagree, the robust row is the one that counts.

V1 RESULT: the pre-committed manipulation test REJECTS the null of a continuous density at the pass mark (p = 0.0135 < 0.05). The estimated density is HIGHER just above 40 than just below it, which is the direction that marking to the boundary would produce: borderline scripts pushed up to a pass rather than left just below one.
The heaping table above is reported because Section 12 asks for it, and it shows that 40 is not the only spike: every multiple of five is elevated. It is not, however, a reason to set the test aside. The test was pre-committed, it was run as specified, and it rejected. Reading the heaping table as a reason to keep the design would be choosing the criterion after seeing the result, which is the practice this protocol exists to prevent.

==========================

Scores within 1 mark of the pass mark are excluded, in case exact-40 scores are administratively assigned rather than earned. Given the spike at exactly 40 in the V1 table, this drops the mass point itself along with its immediate neighbours.

Reported as measured: the bias-corrected point estimate moves from -0.1284 on the full sample to -0.0248 once the 201 rows at scores 39 to 41 are removed, and the robust interval widens accordingly. Section 12 pre-committed to running this specification and to reporting it whatever it showed; it did not state how to read a difference of this kind, and no rule for reading one is invented here. What can be said without a criterion is factual: most of what the full-sample estimate measures comes from the rows at and immediately around the mass point that V1 flags.


8. Power analysis
=================

Independent of what the RD found. Section 12 asks what a randomised trial would need in order to detect an intervention effect of a given size, which is the artefact that answers 'how would you design the experiment' without pretending one was run.

Two-sided two-proportion test, alpha = 0.05, power = 80%, control non-completion rate = 0.3825 (the observed rate in the Arm 2 population). The effect is a reduction in that rate, in percentage points.

Minimum detectable effect of the RD design itself, at the primary bandwidth:
  effective sample size          247 left, 792 right (1039 total)
  MDE on conventional             0.1373 (13.7 percentage points), from SE = 0.0490

This is what makes a null interpretable. An effect smaller than the MDE would not have been detected by this design at this bandwidth whether or not it exists, so a null here is a statement about what the design could see, not a statement that the effect is zero.


9. What this does not establish
===============================

Section 12 states the limitation and Section 14 L1 repeats it: this is a local effect at one score boundary, among students who sat and were marked on the first assessment. It says nothing about students who never submitted, and those are the highest-risk group in Arm 1. The size of that gap is a number, not a caveat:

  group                                                      n    share  not_completed
  Arm 1 cohort at D=28 (all splits)                      27530   1.0000         0.4414
    of which also in the Arm 2 population                23637   0.8586         0.3752
    of which NOT in the Arm 2 population                  3893   0.1414         0.8433

  Arm 2 rows absent from the Arm 1 D=28 cohort: 279 (removed by E1, E2 or E3 there, but retained here because Arm 2 applies no such exclusion).

3893 of the 27530 students the Arm 1 model scores at D=28 (14.1%) are outside the Arm 2 population, and their non-completion rate is 0.8433 against 0.3752 for those inside it. The population Arm 2 can speak to is the one that already engaged enough to be marked; the students the early warning system most needs to reach are, by construction, the ones this design cannot see. That disconnect is not resolved here and is not resolvable within this design.


10. Declared outcomes (Section 13)
==================================

Unlike O3 to O6, these three are decidable as the protocol wrote them. O7 and O8 turn on statistical significance, which Section 12 fixes by specifying robust bias-corrected inference, and O9 turns on whether V1 fails, which V1 is a test of. No numeric convention has to be invented to reach a verdict here, and none is.

O9 MET: V1 fails. The pre-committed manipulation test rejects a continuous density at the pass mark (jackknife t = 2.4704, p = 0.0135), with excess mass just ABOVE 40. Section 12 declared in advance that if markers push borderline students up to 40, 'the design is compromised and the result is reported as invalid rather than quietly retained'. It is so reported.

The consequence, stated plainly. The identifying assumption of this design is that which side of 40 a student lands on is as good as random for students near the line. If scripts near the line are being moved across it, then which side a student lands on reflects a marker's judgment about that student, and the comparison is no longer between similar students who differ by luck. The estimates in Section 3 are printed because the protocol requires every pre-committed quantity to be reported whatever it shows, NOT because they are interpretable as causal effects. They are not.

For the record and not as a finding: the primary estimate at bandwidth 10 is -0.1284 with robust 95% CI [-0.2803, +0.0234], p = 0.0973. Under the pre-committed inference this interval contains zero, so even taking the design at face value it would not have supported O7.

Per Section 12's declared outcome O9, Arm 2 is reduced to the power analysis alone. The power analysis in Section 8 stands: it does not depend on the RD being valid, only on the observed outcome variance in this population, and it is the part of Arm 2 that survives.

No bandwidth search, no alternative cutoff and no respecification was attempted to rescue the design. The specification run here is the one Section 12 fixed in advance.
